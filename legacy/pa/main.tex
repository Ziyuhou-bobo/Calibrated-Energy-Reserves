\begin{abstract}
\noindent Parcel delivery by uncrewed aircraft is limited by rare events rather than by average performance, and the prevailing response in the learning literature is to optimise a tail statistic of the return distribution. We test whether that helps. We build a stochastic multi-UAV delivery environment with wind, turbulence and gusts, satellite-navigation drift and multipath, cooperative and non-cooperative traffic, and uncertain battery energy, payload and propulsion faults, in which four shift families can be applied independently at test time, and we train six policies under a matched budget with three seeds each, evaluating them on paired scenarios. Three tail-risk designs, including a hazard-adaptive risk level driven by collision threat, energy margin, traffic density and disturbance intensity, do not deliver a reliable reduction in catastrophic losses relative to a risk-neutral soft actor-critic: the gaps stay within bootstrap intervals, change sign across conditions and match the spread across training seeds. Moving the deployment risk level towards caution leaves collision rates unchanged. An online conformal return-to-home reserve, by contrast, holds its specified coverage of return-flight energy in every condition, where fixed margins lose a third of theirs, and removes energy depletion under a battery and payload shift. We quantify the resulting abort--depletion frontier and identify undetected aircraft conflicts as the dominant residual risk.
\end{abstract}
